    Our research direction is built around three habits. Simple to say. Worth getting right.

    TRACK

    the search.

    The winner is only one part of the story. Keep the attempts, choices and assumptions that led to it.

    TEST

    beyond the fit.

    Ask how a model behaves on unseen data, with realistic costs and a process that respects time.

    QUESTION

    the result.

    Look at uncertainty and the number of trials. A strong backtest earns attention, not automatic trust.

    Before you trust a backtest.

    Six questions. Switch on the ones the result can answer.

    Every question a result can answer moves the needle away from luck. A result that answers none of them is most likely a fluke.

    • Do you know how many versions were tried before this one?
    • Was some data kept aside and used only once, at the very end?
    • Does the test only move forward in time, never peeking ahead?
    • Are trading costs and slippage included?
    • Is the result adjusted for the number of trials, for example with a Deflated Sharpe Ratio?
    • Could someone reproduce it from the record of attempts?

    Questions,answered.

    Is Fluke investment advice?

    No. Fluke is research about how trading models are evaluated. Nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

    Are the charts on this site real results?

    No. Every chart here is illustrative and drawn from synthetic data, to show how picking the best of many trials can flatter a result.

    What are the Deflated Sharpe Ratio and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting?

    Two published methods for judging a result in light of the search that produced it. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio adjusts a performance measure for the number of trials and the shape of returns. The Probability of Backtest Overfitting estimates how often the best choice on past data falls behind on new data.

    Who is behind Fluke?

    Fluke is an independent, early-stage research project led by its founder. The notes come first; the tools follow the research.

    The question behind the project

    AN EDGE. OR A FLUKE?

    A strong backtest is the beginning of a conversation. It shouldn’t be the end of one.

    AI makes it easier to explore models, features and trading rules. But more experiments also mean more opportunities to find a result that looks good by chance. If we only see the winner, we lose the context needed to judge it.

    Look at the search, not just the selection.

    What was tried before this model? How many alternatives were discarded? Which decisions changed after someone saw the results? These questions shape the idea behind Fluke.

    Give uncertainty a seat at the table.

    The research explores ways to account for multiple trials and backtest overfitting, including the Deflated Sharpe Ratio and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting. These are tools for asking sharper questions, not certificates of future performance.

    Make the evidence easier to inspect.

    The ambition is to bring the research process into view: the attempts, the validation approach, the costs and the assumptions. So confidence has a reason behind it.
