Look beyond the backtest

Great results get attention. Good questions get closer to the truth.

When you try enough models, one can look extraordinary by chance. Fluke starts with a simple idea: to understand the winner, look at the whole search. Wallets work the same way: with tens of thousands of active accounts, some look brilliant by chance.

One winner. A bigger story.

It’s easy to fall in love with a line. Let’s see what sits behind it.

Twenty-four versions of a model were tried and only the best one was shown. Seen together, the winner is simply the luckiest of the lot. Illustrative demo: the curves are synthetic, not market data.

Same logic, now for wallets.

We study the whole crowd, not just the winner.

    Conviction.
    With a paper trail.

    Every wallet we test goes through three habits. Simple to say. Worth getting right.

    TEST

    the skill.

    Flip each bet’s direction at random, 10,000 times. Keep size, price, timing and market fixed. See how often chance beats the real result.

    CHECK

    it lasts.

    One pass is not enough. A wallet must pass again, on events it hasn’t seen.

    FLAG,

    never accuse.

    A high leakage score means “suspicious”, never “insider”. It is a reason to look closer, not a verdict.

    How the test works.

    Four steps, from public trades to a result you can check.

    Step 1 of 4Illustrative chart

    Data.

    We collect every public trade a wallet made: the market, the side, the size, the price and the time.

    Step 2 of 4Illustrative chart

    Skill test.

    We flip the direction of each bet at random, 10,000 times, and count how often chance beats the real result.

    Step 3 of 4Illustrative chart

    Persistence check.

    A wallet that passes must pass again on events it has not seen, or it does not count.

    Step 4 of 4Illustrative chart

    Multiple-testing correction.

    Test tens of thousands of wallets and some pass by luck alone, so the bar goes up for everyone.

    What research says.

    Three findings behind the project. Each number comes with its source and its status.

    Polymarket, 1.72 million accountsReported

    About 3%

    About 54,000 of 1.72 million accounts pass a skill test. Only accounts active in at least 10 events were tested.

    Read the study Gómez-Cram, Guo, Jensen and Kung (2026), as reported by Nechepurenko (2026). Not re-checked by us.
    Bubblemaps, May 2026Reported

    98% wins

    Nine linked accounts, about $2.4 million in profit, a 98% win rate. We don’t know if these were insiders. That’s why we test.

    Read the report Bubblemaps findings, as reported by Cointelegraph on 19 May 2026. Not re-checked by us.
    Chance, at scaleComputed by us

    2,500 by luck

    Test 50,000 wallets at 5% significance and about 2,500 pass by chance alone, even if none of them has any skill.

    Read on corrections 50,000 × 0.05 = 2,500. The fix for this is a multiple-testing correction, such as Benjamini and Hochberg (1995).

    Honest limits.

    Where it started.

    Before you trust a backtest: six questions. Switch on the ones the result can answer.

    The tool from article 1, The Best Crypto Trading AI Might Just Be the Luckiest. Every question a result can answer moves the needle away from luck. A result that answers none of them is most likely a fluke.

    • Do you know how many versions were tried before this one?
    • Was some data kept aside and used only once, at the very end?
    • Does the test only move forward in time, never peeking ahead?
    • Are trading costs and slippage included?
    • Is the result adjusted for the number of trials, for example with a Deflated Sharpe Ratio?
    • Could someone reproduce it from the record of attempts?

    Read the thinking behind Fluke

    Questions,answered.

    Is Fluke investment advice?

    No. Fluke is research only. Nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy, sell or bet on anything.

    Does Fluke trade or hold money?

    No, never. No keys, no wallets, no funds. Fluke does not trade and never asks for exchange keys.

    Are you saying these wallets cheat?

    No. A result passes or fails a skill test, nothing more. A high leakage score is a reason to look closer, not evidence of anything.

    What is a prediction market?

    A market where people trade on the outcome of future events, like an election or a match. On Polymarket every trade is recorded on a public blockchain, which is what makes a public test possible.

    What is the Deflated Sharpe Ratio?

    A published method for judging a result in light of the search that produced it: it adjusts a performance measure for the number of trials and the shape of returns. It is the tool behind article 1 and the Fluke Check. Its cousin, the Probability of Backtest Overfitting, estimates how often the best choice on past data falls behind on new data.

    Where does the data come from?

    Public on-chain data: Polymarket trades recorded on Polygon, read through the open dataset SII-WANGZJ/Polymarket_data on Hugging Face. Nothing private, and no guesses about who is behind a wallet.

    Who is behind it?

    An independent, student-led research project, early-stage. The thinking is in the open: start with article 1 on Medium and the research note on this page. Article 2 is in progress.

    Research note No. 2

    From bots to wallets: why prediction markets

    AN EDGE. OR A FLUKE?

    A winning wallet is the beginning of a question. It shouldn’t be the end of one.

    Fluke began with AI trading models: how do you judge one by the whole search behind it, and not just by its best backtest? That was article 1, The Best Crypto Trading AI Might Just Be the Luckiest.

    Why prediction markets.

    The same question fits prediction markets, and there the evidence is public. On Polymarket every bet sits on-chain. Some wallets win a lot. Is that skill, luck, or something worth a closer look? As far as we could find, there is no public, reproducible skill test for wallets. That is a gap, not a money decision. Fluke does not trade, hold funds or ask for exchange keys.

    Look at the search, not just the selection.

    What was tried before this model? How many alternatives were discarded? With wallets, the search is the crowd: among tens of thousands of accounts, some will win by chance. These questions shape the idea behind Fluke.

    Give uncertainty a seat at the table.

    The research explores ways to account for multiple trials and overfitting: the Deflated Sharpe Ratio and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting for models, a sign-flip skill test and a multiple-testing correction for wallets. These are tools for asking sharper questions, not certificates of future performance.

    Make the evidence easier to inspect.

    The ambition is to bring the research process into view: the data, the method, the tests and their limits. So confidence, or suspicion, has a reason behind it.
